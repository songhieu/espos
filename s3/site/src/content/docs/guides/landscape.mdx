An EspOS device can stand four ways up. Your app draws in **320 × 480** in portrait and **480 × 320** in landscape, on both boards. Most apps work in both without a change; `app.screen` and half cards let you use the wide screen well.

## The four orientations

| `s3.rot` | Name on the device | Screen | The USB-C port |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0` | Portrait | 320 × 480 | at the bottom |
| `1` | Landscape | 480 × 320 | on the left |
| `2` | Portrait, upside down | 320 × 480 | at the top |
| `3` | Landscape, upside down | 480 × 320 | on the right |

The boards have no tilt sensor, so the screen turns only when the user picks a way up:

- **Settings › Display**: four pictures of the device, with the USB-C port drawn on each. A tap turns the screen at once.
- **Control Center › Rotate**: switches between the user's portrait way and landscape way (the last one used of each), so it never turns the screen upside down by surprise. Touch and hold opens Settings › Display.
- **The EspOS web**: the device's settings. The setting `s3.rot` syncs both ways, so the web can set it before the device is unpacked.

A case that stands the board on its side with the USB-C port on the right uses rotation `3`.

## What happens when the screen turns

<Steps>

1. **Every screen is built again** for the new shape, as after a change of look. The keyboard and any open sheet close.
2. **The app in front opens again**, with the same `app.arg`. Your `main.js` runs from the top in a fresh context, so variables start over. What you saved with `store` is still there.
3. **Home shows its own pages.** In landscape, Home has its own pages (the setting `s3.home_l`): a grid of 5 × 4 cells with the dock on the right. They start as a copy laid out from the portrait pages; after that each orientation is edited on its own. Settings › Home Screen › "Lay out landscape from portrait again" copies them again.

</Steps>

Games follow the same rule: PawBoop comes back with her saved state, and a Java game is asked to save (its `destroyApp` runs) before it opens again in the new shape.

<Aside type="tip" title="Save state as it changes">
A turn closes and reopens your app, like Back and opening it again. An app that saves as it goes and keeps times as timestamps (`Date.now()`) comes back exactly where it was. See [App lifecycle](/concepts/lifecycle/).
</Aside>

## `app.screen`

```ts
app.screen?: {w: number; h: number; landscape: boolean}
```

The room the app draws in: `{w: 320, h: 480, landscape: false}` in portrait, `{w: 480, h: 320, landscape: true}` in landscape. It is set before `main.js` runs, and since a turn opens the app again, it is never out of date while the app runs.

Under the title bar your app has:

| | Portrait | Landscape |
|---|---|---|
| Width for content | 296 px | 456 px |
| Height under the title bar | 412 px | 252 px |
| A half card (`{half: true}`) | the whole line, 296 px | 224 px, two on a line |

The screen scrolls when the content is taller, in both orientations.

## Half cards

`ui.card(children, {half: true})` is half the line in landscape and the whole line in portrait. Put two half cards in a `ui.row`: side by side in landscape, one under the other in portrait, from the same code.

```js title="main.js"
// Two half cards: side by side in landscape, one under the other in portrait.
const {w, h, landscape} = app.screen;
let watered = store.get('watered', Date.now() - 2 * 86400000);

function water() {
  watered = Date.now();
  store.set('watered', watered);
}

app.title = 'Plant';
app.render = () => {
  const days = Math.floor((Date.now() - watered) / 86400000);
  const shape = `${w} x ${h}, ${landscape ? 'landscape' : 'portrait'}`;
  return ui.column([
    ui.row([
      ui.card([
        ui.text('Soil', {dim: true}),
        ui.big('42%', {color: '#16a34a'}),
        ui.progress(0.42),
      ], {half: true, center: true}),
      ui.card([
        ui.text('Last watered', {dim: true}),
        ui.title(days === 0 ? 'Today' : `${days} days ago`),
        ui.text('Water again when the soil is under 30%.'),
      ], {half: true}),
    ]),
    ui.button('Watered now', water, {wide: true, bg: '#16a34a'}),
    ui.text(shape, {dim: true, size: 13}),
  ]);
};
```

## Designing for both

- **Start from portrait.** One column that reads well at 296 px also works at 456 px. Most apps need nothing more.
- **Pair what belongs together.** A value and its details, a timer and today's count: two half cards in a row. The Focus app in the store puts its timer and the day's sessions side by side this way, and keeps its buttons under them.
- **Mind the height in landscape.** 252 px holds about one card and a row of buttons. Put the main action near the top, or in a half card, so it shows without scrolling.
- **Choose a different tree when it helps.** `render` can return another layout for `app.screen.landscape`, for example the chips and buttons on top in landscape and at the bottom in portrait. Keep the state the same, so a turn changes only the layout.
- **Big digits fit a half card.** Focus shows its `25:00` at 64 px in a half card.
- **Lists keep the whole line.** `ui.list` rows are as wide as their container in both orientations; for two short lists side by side, put each in a half card.
- **Do not depend on pixel sizes.** Lay out with columns, rows and cards, and read `app.screen` only to choose between layouts.

## The 2.8-inch board

The ES3C28P shows the same interface at 2/3. In portrait the 320 × 480 picture becomes 213 × 320 in the middle of its 240 × 320 panel. In landscape the 480 × 320 picture becomes 320 × 213 across the full width of the turned panel, with 13 black rows above and 14 below. Apps get the same `app.screen` on both boards and need nothing special.

<Shot src={display28} alt="The 2.8-inch board in QEMU, landscape upside down: the Display settings at 2/3, with black bands above and below" caption="The 2.8-inch board in QEMU, rotation 3: 320 × 213 on the 320 × 240 panel" />

At 2/3, 12 px text becomes 8 px on the glass. Prefer 13 px and up, and buttons at least 44 px high (the `ui.button` default). The 2.8-inch board's landscape has run in QEMU; it has not yet been seen on a real board.

## Test both orientations

- **Simulator script:** `rot N` turns the screen and opens the app in front again. With the CLI, put it in the script:

  ```txt title="sim/land.txt"
  rot 1
  wait 800
  shot landscape
  rot 0
  wait 800
  shot portrait
  ```

  ```sh
  espos sim --headless -s sim/land.txt
  ```

- **Live simulator:** type `rot 1` in its terminal.
- **QEMU and a board:** the dev console's `ROT N`, or Settings › Display. `espos device tap` takes coordinates of the current orientation.

The commands are in [Simulator commands](/reference/sim-commands/) and [Dev console commands](/reference/dev-console/).
