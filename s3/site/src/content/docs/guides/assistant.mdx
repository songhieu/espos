An **assistant app** answers when the person says the wake word on their EspOS device. You write its brain (your own
model, a cloud API, rules, anything) and run it as a small web service: your **provider**. EspOS sends it each turn
as one signed HTTPS request and speaks your answer in the voice of the dog the person chose.

<Aside type="caution" title="Preview">
The protocol is v1 and stable, the tools on this page work today, and your own assistant apps can be set up on
espos.dev. Devices sending their turns to third-party providers is switched off on the hosted service until it has
been reviewed: test with `espos ai test` until then.
</Aside>

| You write | EspOS does |
|---|---|
| A provider: one HTTPS endpoint that answers a JSON turn with a JSON answer | Hears the person, writes down what they said, sends the turn, speaks the answer |
| An assistant app: a manifest with `"provides": ["assistant"]`, your endpoint and permissions | Installs it, shows its permissions, lets the person pick it as the assistant |
| What to say and what to do (set an alarm, add a note) | Checks every action against what the person allowed, then does it, or says it could not |

## How a turn goes

<Steps>

1. The person says the wake word and asks something. The device records it; the EspOS server turns it into text.
2. The server builds the **turn request**: what was said, the time and place, and only the data your app's
   permissions allow (the weather, the alarms, the memories, and so on). It signs it with your app's secret and sends it to
   your endpoint.
3. Your provider answers with what to **say**, and optionally **actions** (set an alarm, add a to-do) and **memory**
   (remember that the person likes tea).
4. The server checks the answer: actions you were not allowed, or that do not fit, are refused, and then none of
   your actions run. It does what is left and the device speaks your words.

</Steps>

Nothing on the device changes for your app: the server talks to your provider, the device talks to the server.

## From a template to a passing provider

<Steps>

1. **Make the app.** The template has a small screen for the device and a working provider in `provider/server.mjs`
   (Node 20, about 80 lines, no dependencies).

   ```sh
   espos create home-ai --template assistant --id vn.you.homeai --name "Home AI"
   cd home-ai
   ```

2. **Run the provider** on your machine with any secret that starts with `esps_`.

   ```sh
   export ESPOS_SIGNING_SECRET=esps_try_it_locally_0123456789abcdef
   node provider/server.mjs
   ```

   ```txt title="Output"
   Home AI answers on http://localhost:8787/espos/turn
   ```

3. **Test it** from a second terminal. `espos ai test` sends signed example turns and checks every answer with the
   server's own rules.

   ```sh
   ESPOS_SIGNING_SECRET=esps_try_it_locally_0123456789abcdef espos ai test http://localhost:8787/espos/turn
   ```

   ```txt title="Output"
     manifest                         ok             protocol 1
     turn: weather                    ok       5 ms  mood happy
     turn: set an alarm               ok       2 ms  mood happy, 1 action(s) alarm.add
     turn: nothing heard              ok       2 ms  mood happy
     turn: held delete                ok       2 ms  mood happy
     signature: a wrong one refused   ok       2 ms  refused (401)
   6 of 6 passed
   ```

4. **Make it yours.** Replace `answer()` in `provider/server.mjs` with your model. Keep the signature check, and
   answer only from the request: the turn carries everything you may know.

5. **Put it online** behind https on a public host, and point the manifest's `assistant.provider` at it.

6. **Upload the app and set it up on espos.dev.** The app installs on your own devices
   ([Your app on your own device](/guides/my-apps/)); the secret is made on your machine, sent once and shown once.

   ```sh
   espos upload
   espos ai secret u.vn.you.homeai --endpoint https://ai.example.com/espos/turn
   ```

   Put the secret in your provider as `ESPOS_SIGNING_SECRET`. `espos ai status` shows the last day's turns and
   failures; `espos ai pause` and `espos ai resume` stop and start the turns.

</Steps>

A finished example lives in the repository: [`s3/examples/assistant-echo`](https://github.com/songhieu/espos/tree/main/s3/examples/assistant-echo),
made from the same template, with its provider in `provider/server.mjs`.

## The manifest

```json title="manifest.json"
{
  "id": "vn.you.homeai",
  "name": "Home AI",
  "version": "1.0.0",
  "code": 10000,
  "kind": "js",
  "engine": "js",
  "provides": ["assistant"],
  "assistant": { "provider": "https://ai.example.com/espos/turn", "languages": ["*"] },
  "perms": ["location:city", "data:weather", "assist.alarms", "alarms.write"]
}
```

- `provides: ["assistant"]` makes it an assistant: the person can pick it in **Settings › Default apps › Assistant**.
- `assistant.provider` is your endpoint: https, on a public host.
- `perms` are what the server may send your provider and do for it. The person sees them at install and can switch
  them off later; a permission switched off stops at the next turn.

`espos check` refuses an assistant permission in an app that is not an assistant (M049) and an assistant without an
https provider (M050).

## Permissions

Every turn carries what was said (`turn.text`) and the language, time zone and time (`locale`). Everything else comes
only with a permission.

| Permission | What your provider gets or may do | Request scope |
|---|---|---|
| (always) | What the person said; the language, time zone and time | `turn.text`, `locale` |
| `assist.audio` | The recording of the voice (a voice can identify a person) | `turn.audio` |
| `assist.name` | The name the person wants to be called | `profile.name` |
| `location:city` | The device's city (never coordinates or an address) | `place` |
| `data:weather` | The weather there | `weather` |
| `data:calendar`, `data:markets`, `data:news`, `data:reading` | The calendar, market prices, news headlines, reading time | `calendar`, `markets`, `news`, `reading` |
| `assist.alarms`, `assist.todo`, `assist.notes` | The alarms, to-dos and note titles | `alarms`, `todo`, `notes` |
| `assist.notes.body` | The notes with their text | `notes.body` |
| `assist.memory`, `assist.history` | What the person asked to be remembered; earlier conversations | `memories`, `history` |
| `assist.desk` | The state of the person's computer (EspOS Desktop) | `desk` |
| `alarms.write`, `todo.write`, `notes.write` | Set alarms, to-dos, notes | `act.alarms`, `act.todo`, `act.notes` |
| `settings.write` | Change a few device settings (volume, brightness, quiet hours, and so on) | `act.settings` |
| `memory.write` | Remember and forget things; save the person's name | `act.memory` |
| `desk.control` | Run the computer commands the person turned on | `act.desk` |

The same table, as data: [`assistant-v1-scopes.json`](https://github.com/songhieu/espos/blob/main/s3/sdk/packages/sdk/schemas/assistant-v1-scopes.json).
A field your app was not granted is simply missing from the request.

## The turn and the answer

A turn, shortened (the full example and the schema are linked below):

```json title="POST /espos/turn"
{
  "v": "1",
  "type": "turn",
  "request_id": "req_01JDA7Q3V8K2",
  "turn": { "id": "t_9c1e", "seq": 7, "source": "voice", "input": { "text": "Set an alarm for 6:30 tomorrow" } },
  "locale": { "language": "en", "timezone": "Europe/Berlin", "utc_offset_min": 60 },
  "persona": { "id": "pawboop/bao", "name": "Bao" },
  "scopes": ["turn.text", "locale", "place", "weather", "alarms", "act.alarms"],
  "context": {
    "place": { "city": "Berlin" },
    "weather": { "state": "fresh", "fetched_ms": 1795299400000, "days": [{ "date": "2026-11-21", "min_c": 2, "max_c": 6, "text": "light rain" }] },
    "alarms": { "snapshot": "h_3a9c", "items": [{ "ref": "al_1", "time": "07:00", "days": "12345", "on": true }] }
  },
  "capabilities": { "actions": ["alarm.add", "alarm.update", "alarm.delete"], "moods": ["happy", "alert"] }
}
```

```json title="Your answer"
{
  "v": "1",
  "request_id": "req_01JDA7Q3V8K2",
  "heard": "Set an alarm for 6:30 tomorrow",
  "say": { "text": "Alarm set for 6:30 tomorrow.", "if_not_done": "I couldn't set that alarm." },
  "actions": [{ "id": "a1", "type": "alarm.add", "args": { "time": "06:30", "days": "" } }]
}
```

- Answer the same `request_id`, and always something to `say`. Without it, the turn counts as not answered.
- Lists come with **refs** (`al_1`, `td_2`, `nt_1`, `m_3`): change or delete an item by its ref, never by its text.
- Deleting two or more things at once is **held**: the device asks the person first. The next turn says so in
  `held`, and you answer `"held": {"actions": "yes"}` when the person agrees.
- To forget a memory, give its ref, or the words it holds: `{"op": "forget", "match": "the dentist"}`.
- `say.if_not_done` is what the device says instead when one of your actions was refused.
- At most 30 actions and 20 memory changes in one answer, and at most 64 KB.

## Signing and limits

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Signature | `EspOS-Signature: t=<unix seconds>,v1=<hex HMAC-SHA256(secret, t + "." + request_id + "." + body)>` |
| Your check | refuse a timestamp more than 5 minutes off, a `request_id` seen in the last 10 minutes, a wrong signature (401) |
| Secret rotation | a new secret keeps the previous one valid for 24 hours; both signatures come in the header |
| Deadline | a complete answer within 12 seconds |
| Redirects | not followed: answer at the endpoint itself |
| After failures | 3 failures in 5 minutes rest your app for 2 minutes; a 429 rests it for its `retry_after_s` |
| Privacy | the person and the device come as refs of your app only (two apps cannot match a person); never a device token, id, e-mail or account id |

The signature check in [`provider/server.mjs`](https://github.com/songhieu/espos/blob/main/s3/examples/assistant-echo/provider/server.mjs)
is the one to copy.

- **PawBoop AI is built the same way.** The dog's own brain gets the same request and gives the same answer as yours,
  in [`assist_reply/talk/talk.go`](https://github.com/songhieu/espos/blob/main/server/internal/flows/assist_reply/talk/talk.go):

  | Stage | Function | What it does |
  |---|---|---|
  | Context | `eapRequest` | builds the turn request from the person's data (stale figures left out, lists by ref) |
  | Scopes | `eapScopesFor`, `eapFilter` | keeps only what the app's permissions allow |
  | Brain | `pbSystem`, `pbResponse` | PawBoop's prompt from the request; its answer as a v1 answer |
  | Policy | `eapClean`, then the hold and memory rules in `Run` | checks an answer, any provider's, and does what is allowed |
  | Your provider | `provider_call` (the `provider_call` flow) | signs, sends, times out, rests after failures |

- The whole design, with the decisions behind it: [`s3/docs/assistant-providers.md`](https://github.com/songhieu/espos/blob/main/s3/docs/assistant-providers.md).
