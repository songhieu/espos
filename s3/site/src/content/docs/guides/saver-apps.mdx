A **screen saver app** draws the screen while the device is not being used. The person picks it in **Settings,
Default apps, Screen saver**. From then on the device runs your `saver.js` whenever the saver comes on, and a touch
brings them back to where they were.

<Aside type="caution" title="Preview">
Saver apps need firmware 0.6.1 or later (`"min_fw": 601`). The drawing calls on this page are API level 2 and may
still grow; what is here will keep working.
</Aside>

| You write | EspOS does |
|---|---|
| `saver.js`: what one frame looks like (`app.onFrame`) | Decides **when** the saver shows: after how long, the night hours, the brightness (Settings, Screen saver) |
| A manifest with `"provides": ["saver"]` | Lists your app in Default apps and in the Store's "Screen savers" |
| Optional settings (colours, speed) | Draws their form on the device and on the web, and hands you the values |
| | Leaves the saver on a touch, shows its own night page at night, stops a saver that misbehaves |

## From the sample to your device

<Steps>

1. Copy the sample `examples/saver-bubbles` (bubbles rise behind the time) and change its `id` in `manifest.json`.
2. `espos check` and `espos build`: the package is `dist/<id>-1.0.0-core.espk`.
3. Try it in the simulator: `espos sim --headless --script sim/saver.txt` picks it as the saver, takes two frames and
   touches the screen. The shots go to `.espos/shots`.
4. `espos upload` puts it on your own devices ([Your app on your own device](/guides/my-apps/)). On the device:
   Settings, Default apps, Screen saver, then your app. **Preview** on the same page shows it at once.

</Steps>

## The manifest

```json
{
  "id": "com.example.starfield",
  "name": "Starfield",
  "version": "1.0.0", "code": 10000,
  "kind": "js", "engine": "js",
  "min_fw": 601,
  "provides": ["saver"],
  "entry": {"saver": "saver.js", "app": "main.js"},
  "settings": [{"key": "speed", "type": "number", "label": "Speed", "min": 1, "max": 10, "default": 4}]
}
```

- `provides: ["saver"]` is what makes it a saver app.
- The saver's file is `entry.saver`. Without it the device looks for `saver.js`, then `main.js`.
- `main.js` is still the app on Home. A short page saying what it is and opening its settings is enough.
- List `saver.js` under `files` in `espos.json` so `espos build` packs it.

## Drawing a frame

```js
let x = 40, y = 40, dx = 2, dy = 3;
app.onStart = (info) => {      // once, before the first frame: info.w, info.h, info.landscape
  app.fps(15);                 // 1 to 20 frames a second (10 when you do not ask)
};
app.onFrame = (g, dt) => {      // dt: ms since the last frame
  g.clear('#081020');
  g.circle(x, y, 18, '#ffcc33');
  g.text(app.time('HH:mm'), g.w / 2, g.h - 120, {size: 64, color: '#ffffff', align: 'center', bold: true});
  x += dx, y += dy;
  if (x < 18 || x > g.w - 18) dx = -dx;
  if (y < 18 || y > g.h - 18) dy = -dy;
};
```

Each frame starts empty and is shown whole once `onFrame` returns. **A frame that draws nothing keeps the last one on
the screen**: a clock that changes once a minute can return early the rest of the time and costs almost nothing.

| Call | What it draws |
|---|---|
| `g.clear(c)` | fills the frame and forgets what was drawn before in it |
| `g.rect(x, y, w, h, c, {r, a})` | a rectangle; `r` rounds its corners, `a` is the opacity 0..1 |
| `g.circle(x, y, r, c, {a})` | a disc centred on x, y |
| `g.line(x1, y1, x2, y2, c, {w, a})` | a line `w` pixels wide (1..64, default 2) with round ends |
| `g.gradient(x, y, w, h, c1, c2, {dir})` | from `c1` to `c2`, top to bottom (`"v"`) or left to right (`"h"`) |
| `g.text(s, x, y, {size, color, align, bold, a})` | text whose top is at `y`; `align` puts its left edge, middle or right edge at `x` |
| `g.measure(s, {size, bold})` | `{w, h}`: the room the text takes |
| `g.w`, `g.h`, `g.cls`, `g.mono` | the frame's size, its size class, whether the screen is black and white |

Colours are `"#rrggbb"`, `"#rgb"` or a number `0xrrggbb`. Text sizes go from 10 to 96 pixels. Above 24 the sizes have
digits and `: . , + - % °` only (letters fall back to a smaller size), which suits a clock.

<Aside type="tip" title="Move things a little">
A clock that never moves stays on the same pixels all night. Move it a few pixels every minute, like the sample does
(`app.time('mm')` picks the offset).
</Aside>

## What the saver file has

The saver runs in a small runtime of its own, not in your app's. It has:

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| `app.onStart(info)` | the saver starts: `info = {as: "saver", w, h, cls, mono, scale, landscape}` |
| `app.onFrame(g, dt)` | draw one frame |
| `app.onStop()` | the saver goes: a touch, the night, the screen turned |
| `app.onPause()` / `app.onResume()` | the assistant's pop-up came over the saver and went (no frames meanwhile) |
| `app.fps(n)` | frames a second, 1 to 20 |
| `app.time(fmt)` | the device's time: `HH H hh h mm ss a dd d MM M yyyy yy`, e.g. `app.time('dd/MM')` |
| `app.lang` | the language the device shows (`"en"`, `"vi"`...) for your own words |
| `settings.get(key)` | one of your settings (the same values as in your app); read them in `onStart` |
| `log(text)` | a line in the device's trace ("saver app &lt;id&gt; log: ...") |

There is no `ui`, `store`, `net`, `timer` or `sound` in the saver, and it gets no touches: a touch always brings
the person back. JavaScript's own `Math`, `Date`, arrays and classes are all there.

## When it runs

- **When** is the person's choice in Settings, Screen saver: after how many minutes, the brightness, the night hours.
  Your app only draws.
- **At night** (the night hours) the device shows its own dim night page instead of your app, and starts your app again
  in the morning.
- **The screen turned**: your app is stopped and started again with the new `info.w` and `info.h`.
- **A setting changed** on the web or on the device: the new value is there the next time the saver starts.

## The budget

| | Limit |
|---|---|
| Memory | 512 KB for your script's objects |
| One call (`onStart`, `onFrame`...) | 200 ms; the script's first run, 1.5 s |
| Frames | 1 to 20 a second |
| One frame | 600 shapes and 4 KB of text; more are left out |
| `saver.js` | 128 KB |

A saver that **throws**, runs past its time or memory, or takes over 100 ms for three frames in a row is stopped.
The device's own saver ("Clock and photos") shows for the rest of that time. If that happens three times in ten
minutes, your app is not started again and the person gets one notification saying so. Picking it again in Default
apps, or an update of your app, lets it run again. `espos sim --headless` fails on such a stop and prints the reason.

## Test it

- `espos sim --headless --script sim/saver.txt`: the sample's script picks the app with `set slot.saver <id>`, shows
  the saver (`saver`), takes shots and touches the screen. Write your own the same way; `clock 2026-11-22 23:30`
  jumps the clock into the night hours.
- In the s3desk tree, `python3 tools/saver_sim_test.py` runs the sample and five broken savers (one that throws, one
  too slow, one that eats memory, one that does not parse, one that draws too much) and checks what the device does.
